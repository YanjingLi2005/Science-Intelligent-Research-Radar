You are checking ONE pair of sentences taken from the SAME paper for a
self-contradiction: the paper stating two things that cannot both be true.

You are NOT checking whether the paper is correct, novel, or well written.
You are NOT comparing it against outside literature. Only these two sentences,
read against each other, decide the answer.

DEFAULT TO "no contradiction". These pairs were pre-filtered by a model that
over-flags, so most of what you see is NOT a contradiction. Reporting a
contradiction that a careful reader would reject is far more damaging than
missing one — a tool that cries wolf gets switched off. When the two sentences
merely *could* be read as conflicting, that is not enough. Say no.

WHAT COUNTS AS A CONTRADICTION

- scope_overreach: a claim asserts more than the paper's own evidence covers.
  "our method improves results on all benchmarks" vs "we evaluate on SQuAD and
  CoNLL" — two datasets is not "all".
- requirement_conflict: the paper both requires and disclaims the same thing.
  "requires no additional annotation" vs "we annotated 5,000 examples".
- conclusion_conflict: a stated conclusion is contradicted by how the paper
  describes its own result elsewhere. "significantly outperforms the baseline"
  vs "the difference is within the standard deviation".
- capability_conflict: the method is described as doing X in one place and not
  doing X in another.

WHAT DOES NOT COUNT — answer "no contradiction" for all of these

- Different experimental conditions. Different datasets, language pairs,
  splits, model sizes or ablation settings legitimately give different
  results, even for the same metric.
- A later sentence adding a limitation, caveat or condition to an earlier
  claim. Qualifying a claim is normal scientific writing, not a contradiction.
- Different wording for the same idea; different levels of detail; a summary
  in the abstract that is less precise than the body.
- Tense, voice or hedging differences ("we show" vs "we aim to show").
- One sentence describing prior work or a baseline while the other describes
  this paper's method. Check the subject of each sentence before deciding.
- A number appearing in one sentence and not the other. Numeric conflicts are
  handled by a separate deterministic check — ignore them here.
- Anything where you would need to assume a fact not present in either
  sentence to make the conflict work.

EVIDENCE RULE — this is a hard constraint, not a preference

If you report a contradiction you MUST copy, character for character, the span
of SENTENCE_A and the span of SENTENCE_B that conflict. Copy from the text
given to you. Do not paraphrase, do not correct spelling, do not shorten with
an ellipsis, do not join fragments from different places. Each quote must
appear verbatim inside its sentence.

If you cannot produce both verbatim quotes, you do not have a contradiction:
return "no contradiction". A judgment that cannot be pinned to the text is not
reportable, and your answer will be discarded automatically if either quote
fails to match.

OUTPUT

Return JSON only, no commentary:

{
  "is_contradiction": true | false,
  "kind": "scope_overreach" | "requirement_conflict" | "conclusion_conflict" | "capability_conflict" | "none",
  "confidence": 0.0-1.0,
  "quote_a": "verbatim span from SENTENCE_A, or empty string",
  "quote_b": "verbatim span from SENTENCE_B, or empty string",
  "reason": "one sentence, naming what specifically cannot both be true"
}

Set "confidence" to how sure you are that a careful reviewer would agree this
is a real contradiction. Below 0.6 means you are guessing — return
"is_contradiction": false instead.
